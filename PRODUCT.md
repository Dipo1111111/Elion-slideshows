# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Solo TikTok and Instagram creators who post carousel **slideshows** — the person who is
scrolling, thinks "that could be a post", and needs it *now*: hook, slides, caption,
hashtags, exportable backgrounds. They work on phones and post in the native app. They are
fast-moving, impatient with fluff, allergic to hype-speak, and usually posting on a
schedule. They are not looking for a tool to learn — they are looking for the next post.

## Product Purpose

Elion turns the creator's "Brain" — niche, app name, app description, audience, and style
memory — into ready-to-post slideshows: a hook, a short slide sequence, a caption, and
hashtags, exported as background PNGs (1080×1920, text added in the native app) plus
copyable text. Success: a creator signs up, hits generate, and posts in minutes. Elion
supplies the slide backgrounds from a real image library (Pinterest pulls through a
platform-held Apify key, cached for reuse). The MVP lane is narrow on purpose: no
posting, no scheduling, no analytics — just generate, edit, export, post manually.

## Brand Personality

Warm, direct, zero hype. A confident tool that knows the creator is the brand — Elion stays
in the background. Three words: **warm · direct · quiet.** Copy speaks in plain sentences
and says "slideshows" and "slides", never "carousels."

## Anti-references

- Hype-speak SaaS landing pages ("supercharge your content") — the opposite of the voice.
- The 2026 AI default: cream/sand bodies, glassmorphism, gradient text, tiny uppercase
  eyebrows over every section, identical card grids.
- Pink/purple "creator economy" clichés — excluded from the palette by decision.
- Tools that make the creator look generic; the content is the star, chrome stays quiet.

## Design Principles

1. **The tool disappears.** Creators are in a task, not touring a product. Every screen
   serves moving a slideshow closer to their phone.
2. **Ready-to-post is the point.** Generate → review → export/copy is the whole loop;
   nothing competes with it.
3. **Quiet chrome, loud content.** Black & white base with one blue accent; the slideshow
   preview is the most saturated thing on screen.
4. **Warm and direct.** Copy is plain, specific, and never shouts. No tagline — the product
   says it itself.
5. **Slideshow-first language.** "Slideshows" and "slides", always. "Carousel" is banned
   from user-facing copy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA: body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, visible focus, keyboard-operable, semantic
form labels with hints and inline errors, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, touch targets
≥44px (the audience is mobile-first). Color is never the only signal.
